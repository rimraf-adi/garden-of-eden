\chapter{Statistical Tables}

\section{How to read these tables}

Every table gives a \emph{critical value}: the point that leaves a stated area (probability) in the tail.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{One-tail area $\alpha$.} The area in one tail only. Use it for a one-sided test and for a one-sided confidence bound.
  \item \textbf{Two-tail area.} The total area in both tails. A two-tail area of $\alpha$ puts $\alpha/2$ in each tail. Use it for a two-sided test and for a two-sided confidence interval.
  \item \textbf{Conversion.} $\text{two-tail area}=2\times\text{one-tail area}$. For example, one-tail $0.025$ is the same column as two-tail $0.05$.
  \item \textbf{Notation.} $z_\alpha$, $t_{\alpha,\nu}$, $\chi^2_{\alpha,\nu}$ and $f_{\alpha,u,v}$ are the points with \emph{upper-tail} area $\alpha$ ($\nu$, $u$, $v$ are degrees of freedom).
\end{itemize}

\section{$F$ distribution}

\textbf{Critical values $f_{\alpha,u,v}$.} $u$ is the numerator degrees of freedom (across) and $v$ the denominator degrees of freedom (down). Tables give upper-tail points only. Lower-tail points come from the reciprocal rule

\[ f_{1-\alpha,\,u,\,v}=\frac{1}{f_{\alpha,\,v,\,u}}. \]

A two-sided $F$-test at level $\alpha$ uses the one-tail table for $\alpha/2$: for $\alpha=0.05$ use the $0.025$ table; for $\alpha=0.10$ use the $0.05$ table.
